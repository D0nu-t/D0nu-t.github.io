TinyNLA — Natural Language
Autoencoder for SLMs

Testing whether a transformer's residual stream activations can be verbalized into natural language and reconstructed from that description alone. Text-as-description conditioning outperforms heuristic labels in early experiments on GPT-2, though this is single-layer work on one model.

Question

Mechanistic interpretability typically produces technical artifacts: vectors, attention maps, feature directions. These require domain expertise to read. TinyNLA asks a simpler question: can a transformer's internal state at a given layer be described in plain natural language, and can that description carry enough information to reconstruct the original activation?

If yes, natural language becomes a practical interface for interpretability tooling. A practitioner could read a layer description, edit it, and observe the downstream effect without manipulating activation space directly.

Hypothesis: text-as-description conditioning outperforms heuristic activation labels for latent reconstruction because natural language is richer and more compositional than hand-crafted feature descriptors. Early results on GPT-2 layer 1 support this. Whether it holds across layers is the next thing to measure.

Architecture Built

Three stages. A source model (GPT-2 or Qwen2.5-7B) produces per-token residual stream activations at every layer. A Natural Language Autoencoder, distilgpt2-based, maps those activations to a text description. A decoder produces a reconstructed activation, which patches back into the source model at the target layer to measure functional change.

PPL shift is the primary metric: the ratio of perplexity under the patched activation to perplexity under the original. A ratio of 1.0 means functionally identical. Current architecture produces 57x on GPT-2 layer 1, down from approximately 4,600x in the first iteration. The improvement came from fixing three architectural errors: mean pooling across the token dimension (destroyed positional structure), an incompatible encoder (DistilBERT is bidirectional, not causally compatible with autoregressive models), and a positional broadcasting error in the patching step.

57x is a large improvement but still far from 1.0 (functionally identical). The improvement reflects architecture corrections, not training improvements. How much further the ratio can drop with unfrozen encoder layers is the next experiment.

Finding

Text-as-description conditioning produces better reconstructions than heuristic labels on GPT-2 layer 1. When the NLA is prompted with a short description of what a layer is doing rather than a categorical label, both PPL shift and cosine similarity between original and reconstructed activations improve.

The geometry/function decoupling result from the interpretability platform reinforces this. At layer 5, activations with cosine similarity 0.995 produce output distributions with KL divergence around 7. That gap is what the NLA has to bridge, and natural language descriptions carry more signal for bridging it than heuristic labels do.

What this points toward: if activations can be verbalized reliably, a practitioner could inspect, edit, and re-inject model internals in natural language — a debugger for neural computation that operates at the description level rather than the vector level. Current results show the direction is viable. The target is not reached.

Goals

  • Layer-wise sweep — track PPL shift and cosine similarity across all layers of GPT-2 and Qwen2.5, not just layer 1. This will show whether 57x is representative or a best case.
  • Unfreeze encoder layers — test whether additional trainable capacity in the NLA encoder drops the PPL shift further beyond what the architecture corrections achieved.
  • CKA metrics — add Centered Kernel Alignment for a more principled measure of representational alignment between original and reconstructed activations.
  • Layer-5 conditioning sweep — run the full text-as-description vs heuristic comparison at layer 5 where the geometry/function decoupling is strongest.
