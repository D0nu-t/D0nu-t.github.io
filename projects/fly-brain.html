What this shows

The grey cloud is the brain itself: 22,000 neuron positions sampled from the 139,248 neurons annotated in the FlyWire map of one adult female fly. The coloured dots are the 533 neurons of the circuit, joined by 29,111 connections.

Every neuron follows the same simple rule. Its voltage leaks back towards rest, each incoming spike nudges it up or down by 0.275 mV per synapse, and it fires when it crosses a threshold. Whether a neuron excites or inhibits its targets comes from the transmitter predicted for it. The rule and its constants are those of the whole-brain model published by Shiu and colleagues in 2024.

The circuit was cut out of that whole-brain model. I ran the full 138,639-neuron model with the sugar neurons driven, alone and with each of the 24 busiest relay neurons silenced, and kept every neuron that fired or came within 2 mV of firing. Neurons that never fire send nothing, so the cut-down circuit behaves like the whole brain for those experiments. Silencing a neuron outside that list is an approximation.

Time is slowed down. At the slow setting one second on screen is 60 ms in the fly.

How it was checked

The original model runs in the Brian 2 simulator. I rewrote it in plain Python and then in TypeScript for this page. On the published example, the motor neuron fires at 93.3 Hz in the authors' results and 93.7 Hz in my rewrite, and firing rates across all active neurons correlate at 0.999. With the same random seed, the cut-down circuit produces the same spike counts as the whole brain.

Sources and limits

I did not produce the data or the model. I wrote the browser version and this page.

  • Wiring: FlyWire FAFB version 783, Dorkenwald et al., Nature 2024, released under CC BY 4.0 on Zenodo.
  • Model and connection tables: Shiu et al., Nature 2024, from the authors' repository (MIT licence).
  • Neuron positions, types and transmitters: the FlyWire annotations of Schlegel et al., Nature 2024, from the flywire_annotations repository.
  • I found these through Virtual Fly Brain (Court et al., 2023), which indexes fly brain data from many labs.

A wiring map is a structure, and a model built on it is a guess about activity. Here every neuron has the same constants, nothing fires unless it is driven, and the wiring comes from a single animal. The page shows how a signal could travel through this circuit under those assumptions. It is not a recording of a fly.
